\textbf{Equivalent mechanical slosh models.} Sloshing is commonly represented for control design by equivalent mechanical systems, pendulums or mass-spring-dampers, one per mode \cite{capolupo2025equivalent}. Guagliumi et al.\ use a linear and a nonlinear mass-spring-damper model to estimate the sloshing height at the container wall for planar motions \cite{guagliumi2021simple}, and Di Leva et al.\ validate a sloshing-height model for two-dimensional motions of a cylindrical container experimentally \cite{leva2022sloshing}. Our parametric baselines are of this family, and our spill criterion is the same quantity, the surface height at the wall.

\textbf{Slosh-aware planning and control.} Muchacho et al.\ model the end effector as a point mass on a spherical pendulum and obtain slosh-free trajectories from a quadratic program \cite{muchacho2022solution}; Arrizabalaga et al.\ avoid the pendulum model with a geometric, virtual-quadrotor formulation for real-time slosh-free tracking \cite{arrizabalaga2024geometric}. T\'oth et al.\ design fast rest-to-rest feedforward manoeuvres from multi-mode pendulum parameters identified for arbitrary container shapes \cite{toth2024rapid}. Ferrari et al.\ plan time-optimal trajectories that keep the liquid surface below a threshold \cite{ferrari2026time}; Medico et al.\ embed a nonlinear spherical-pendulum slosh model in real-time MPC for containers on a tray \cite{medico2026model}; Hynninen and Kyrki formulate time-optimal spill-free emergency stopping as MPC \cite{hynninen2026emergency}. These works fix the model class and study the planner; we fix the planner and vary the model class.

\textbf{Learned models.} Abderezaei et al.\ use learned dynamics for spill-free transport of open containers in clutter \cite{abderezaei2024clutter}. Bazzi et al.\ report that humans moving a cup of sloshing liquid appear to rely on simplified internal models \cite{bazzi2024simplified}, which is a reminder that a richer model is not automatically the better controller model. Learned models inside sampling-based planners are standard in model-based reinforcement learning, for example probabilistic ensembles with trajectory sampling \cite{chua2018deep}, and Gaussian-process residual models have been combined with MPC \cite{hewing2017cautious}.

\textbf{Sampling-based MPC and adaptation.} MPPI handles arbitrary dynamics and costs, and recent variants improve its sample efficiency or smoothness \cite{asmar2022model,kicki2025lp}; our controller uses smooth, low-pass perturbations in that spirit. Inferring a latent description of the environment from recent history is the mechanism of rapid motor adaptation \cite{kumar2021rma} and of implicit identification for dynamics models \cite{evans2022context}; our history encoder applies it to liquid properties. Input shaping, the convolution of a command with an impulse sequence to cancel residual vibration \cite{kotaniemi2025data}, is our open-loop baseline.
